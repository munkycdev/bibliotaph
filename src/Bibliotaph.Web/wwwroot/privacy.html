b.Bibliotaph

Privacy policy

Effective date: October 25, 2026

Bibliotaph keeps your books where they already are. This policy explains what the Bibliotaph web service at bibliotaph.online stores about you and your library, why, and how to have it deleted. The short version: we store an index of your library so you can search and organise it. We never store copies of your books, never sell anything, and never show ads.

Who we are and what this covers

Bibliotaph is run by munkycdev, an individual in the USA ("we", "us"). This policy covers the website and service at bibliotaph.online, and the sync between that service and the Bibliotaph Windows app.

The Windows app on its own sends us nothing. It keeps everything on your computer unless you sign it in to bibliotaph.online to sync. The Windows app's own AI and OCR run on your computer, unless you point it at a model server you choose.

Bibliotaph is invite-only for now. You can only create an account with an invitation.

What we collect and why

We collect only what the service needs to work. We don't use analytics, advertising or tracking services.

Your books themselves. To index a book, the server downloads it from your storage, reads it, and deletes its copy once it's done, normally within minutes. Your browser may also keep pieces of books it has shown you in its own storage, on your device, so they open faster next time. You can clear that from the browser or from Settings.

We use this information only to provide Bibliotaph to you. We don't use your library or your books to train AI models, and we don't look at your library unless you ask us to help with a problem.

Signing in and connecting your storage

Signing in and storage are separate. You sign in with an account you already have, then connect one or more storage services where your books live. Each service shows you what Bibliotaph is asking for before you agree.

Signing in. Sign-in is run for us by Auth0, a service of Okta. You sign in on its page with an account you already have, such as Microsoft, Google or Apple, or with your email address. We receive only your name, email address and a unique account ID. We never see or store your password; if you sign in with an email address, Auth0 keeps the password, protected, on our behalf. You can link more than one sign-in to the same account.

Connecting storage. You can connect storage services such as OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox, and add folders from each one to your library. For every service, we ask for the least access the service offers:

AccessWhat Bibliotaph does with it
Read your filesList and read the folders you add to your library. Bibliotaph never changes or deletes files in those folders.
Its own folderRead and write one folder that belongs to Bibliotaph, such as Apps/Bibliotaph, where the service offers one. This is your main storage location, where books you import from online libraries are saved.
Write to a folder you chooseAsked for only if you pick a main storage location outside Bibliotaph's own folder. Bibliotaph writes only to that folder.
Keep accessKeep your library up to date while you're not signed in

The exact permission names differ from one service to the next. Settings shows, for each connection, what was granted.

Withdrawing access. Disconnect a storage service in Settings, and we delete its tokens straight away and stop reading from it. You can also remove Bibliotaph from that service's own security or connected-apps settings. Either way, your files aren't touched, and your library keeps what it learned until you remove those folders.

Where a provider has its own rules for apps that use its data, we follow them. For example, if we offer Google Drive, our use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

AI with your own key

Bibliotaph's server uses AI only when you give it your own API key for an AI provider. Bibliotaph never pays for or provides AI itself. Without a key, the server does no AI processing. You can still use AI through the Windows app, which runs a model on your own computer.

When you add a key, the server sends that provider the text it needs to suggest details for your books, such as the title, game system and level range. You choose which books it processes. The provider handles that data under your account and its own terms and privacy policy, not this one. Check whether your provider keeps or trains on API data.

We store your key encrypted, use it only for the requests you set up, and delete it when you remove it or close your account.

Cookies, where data is kept, and who we share it with

Cookies. We use one cookie to keep you signed in, plus short-lived ones that exist only while a sign-in is under way. Auth0's sign-in page sets its own cookies, also only to sign you in. All of them are essential, so we don't ask for consent to them. We use no analytics or advertising cookies. The web app also keeps book pieces and settings in your browser's own storage, on your device.

Where it's kept. Everything we store is in Microsoft Azure data centres in the western United States, including the backups. Auth0 keeps its record of your sign-ins in the United States too. Text recognition (OCR) for scanned books runs on our own server and isn't sent to any third party.

Who we share it with. We don't sell or rent your information, and we don't share it for advertising. Only these parties handle it:

  • Our hosting provider, Microsoft Azure, which runs the service and stores our data on our behalf.
  • Auth0 (Okta), our sign-in service, which confirms who you are on our behalf and keeps a record of your sign-ins.
  • The account you sign in with, such as Microsoft, Google or Apple, which confirms who you are to Auth0.
  • The storage services you connect, which serve your files to Bibliotaph and receive any files it saves for you.
  • The AI provider you choose, only if you add a key (see above).
  • An online store you import from, which receives what's needed to fetch your own purchases, and only when you ask.
  • Authorities, only when the law requires it. We'll tell you unless we're legally prevented.

If Bibliotaph ever changes hands, this policy goes with it, and you'll be told before anything changes.

Closing your account. You can close your account from Settings. We delete your account, library index, your work, tokens and key straight away, and they drop out of backups within 30 days. Nothing in your storage is touched. Books you imported stay in your main storage location, because they're yours.

Your rights. You can:

  • see and export everything we hold about you, from Settings, as a file the Windows app can open;
  • correct it, by editing your library;
  • delete any of it, or all of it by closing your account;
  • object to anything we do with it, or ask us any question, at munkycdev@bibliotaph.online.

We answer requests within 30 days. Depending on where you live, laws such as the GDPR and UK GDPR, or the California Consumer Privacy Act, give you these rights. If you're in the EU or UK, you can also complain to your data protection authority. We process your information to provide the service you signed up for (performance of a contract), and to keep it secure (legitimate interests).

Security, children, changes and contact

Security. All traffic uses HTTPS. Data is encrypted at rest by Azure. Storage tokens and AI keys are also encrypted with a key held in Azure Key Vault, and your browser never sees them. Bibliotaph asks each service only for the narrowest access described above. No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your information, we'll tell you without undue delay, and within 72 hours where the law requires it.

Children. Bibliotaph isn't directed at children under 16, and we don't knowingly hold information about them. If you think a child has an account, contact us and we'll delete it.

Changes. If we change this policy, we'll update the effective date above. If a change affects what we collect or who we share it with, we'll tell you by email or in the app at least 14 days before it applies.
